### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**🌍 Geopolitics & Conflicts: From Fragile Truce to Full-Blown Flare-Up!**
The US-Iran interim peace deal (signed June 15) officially **collapsed** this week. Following Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels in the **Strait of Hormuz** on July 7-8, the US launched retaliatory airstrikes, and President Trump declared the agreement "over." Iran responded with missile and drone attacks on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. The Strait, a critical chokepoint for over a fifth of global seaborne oil and gas, is now subject to a reinstated US naval blockade and potential tolls. UN agencies have called for de-escalation, noting the "near-continuous closure" of the Strait since February has triggered significant commodity price shocks globally.
Beyond the Middle East, the **Venezuela earthquake disaster** continued to worsen, with the death toll rising to 4,333, and 16,740 injured and nearly 18,000 displaced from the June 24 twin earthquakes. The UN appealed for nearly $300 million in aid. Ukrainian attacks on approximately 90 vessels in the Sea of Azov forced Russia to halt commercial shipping and close the Don-Azov canal this week.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Hawkish Fed & Yield Swings!**
Bond markets were on a rollercoaster, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a hawkish Federal Reserve. Inflation reached 4.2% (CPI) and 4.1% (PCE), its highest since 2023. The Fed's July Monetary Policy Report and Chairman Kevin Warsh's comments emphasized a commitment to price stability, with the Fed signaling a strong possibility of one to two more rate hikes by year-end. Futures markets are now pricing in a higher probability of a September Fed hike. US 10-year Treasury yields, after fluctuating, rose to 4.63% today, a two-month high. The US dollar significantly strengthened amidst this volatility and renewed geopolitical risk. Eurozone bond yields also rose following the US and Iranian attacks.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Strait Back in the Spotlight!**
Oil prices experienced extreme whiplash. After initially sliding below $71 a barrel of Brent early in the week on fleeting hopes of Strait normalization, prices **surged dramatically**. By July 8, Brent crude jumped nearly 6% following tanker attacks and Trump's declaration that the peace deal was "over." Today, Brent crude is above $84 a barrel and WTI is near $80, as renewed conflict and the US naval blockade on the Strait of Hormuz fuel intense supply concerns. The US also formally revoked a waiver allowing Iranian oil sales.

**🌾 Agriculture: WASDE Report & Trade Hopes!**
The **USDA's July WASDE report** on Friday, July 10, brought some key updates. New-crop US corn ending stocks were cut below trade expectations, US soybean ending stocks were left unchanged, and US wheat ending stocks were lowered. USDA also slightly raised US soybean production. China's buying of US soybeans continued, offering some market support. Wheat saw gains fueled by renewed speculation of China buying US soybeans and geopolitical news, specifically the Kerch Strait closure. The EU adopted a Delegated Act on July 13, **excluding soybean seed from deforestation regulations**, which aims to ease regulatory uncertainty and support European protein crop production. Crop conditions for spring wheat improved, while corn and soybeans also posted improved conditions after the close, defying trade expectations for declines.

**💰 Metals: Geopolitics & Fed Drive Swings! ✨**
Precious metals saw considerable swings this week. Gold and silver initially saw gains early in the week on hopes of easing Fed rate hike fears, but this reversed sharply. By today, gold prices fell by nearly 3% to around $4,005 per ounce, slipping below the $4,000 mark for the first time since July 1. Silver also saw a notable decline of 2.3% to 4%, while platinum and palladium experienced mixed movements, with some declines due to rate hike fears and industrial demand concerns. Central bank gold purchases, however, continued steadily. In industrial metals, China continues to leverage export controls on rare earth materials. Guardian Metal released positive Pre-Feasibility Study (PFS) results for its Pilot Mountain Tungsten Project in Nevada on June 30, positioning it as a potential new US tungsten source.

The AI race is running at full throttle! The **US government lifted export restrictions on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 AI models** on July 1, allowing for wider global distribution. CIA Director John Ratcliffe reiterated warnings about AI being "digital nuclear weapons," emphasizing the urgency for the agency's adoption of AI and quantum computing. OpenAI is reportedly preparing a confidential IPO filing, with a private-market valuation around $730 billion, though Anthropic has reportedly overtaken OpenAI in revenue. Nvidia reclaimed a $5 trillion market capitalization. Intel announced a significant **€5 billion investment in Ireland** to expand its AI chip production, responding to surging global demand. Fujitsu also launched an AI-driven modernization service to accelerate legacy system transformation, leveraging multiple AI models including Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's GPT. South Korea raised its 2026 economic growth forecast to a five-year high, driven by the AI chip boom. Growing concerns exist about "distillation" – using one AI model's outputs to improve another – highlighting intense competition and IP challenges in the AI space.

This past week has been a stark reminder that global stability is incredibly fragile.
