Iran formally rejected the US response to its seven conditions for reopening the vital waterway, deeming it "insufficient." **💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Inflation's Return & Yield Jitters!**

**US Treasury yields** were on a wild ride, initially surging to multi-decade highs (10-year yield near 5.3% and 30-year near 5.66%) due to inflation concerns and strong US economic data. However, yields pulled back as some Federal Reserve officials hinted at less urgency for immediate rate hikes after the September increase, and before today's CPI data. Today's hotter-than-expected CPI report pushed 10-year yields to a three-week low of 5.1267% and the 30-year to 5.4795%, though comments from Fed officials tempered immediate October hike expectations. Markets are now closely watching upcoming US producer price and retail sales data.

Across the Atlantic, **European government bond yields** similarly climbed, with French 10-year bond yields nearing a 24-year high due to concerns over France's debt burden and political stability. The euro hit a 17-month low against the dollar before recovering slightly.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hustle, Strategic Reserves, & Diesel Tightness!**

**Oil prices** were a dizzying rollercoaster this week, initially surging past $108 a barrel due to escalating Hormuz tensions and tanker attacks, before dipping on President Trump's "energy ceasefire" claims. Brent crude remains above $100. The International Energy Agency (IEA) warned today that diesel supplies will remain "tight" into 2027 due to disruptions and refinery damage from the conflict in the Gulf. OPEC also cut its demand growth forecast but raised next year's, citing the need to replenish oil inventories. The US lifted sanctions on the sale of Russian diesel until April next year amidst an unprecedented price surge. G7 nations agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude from strategic reserves, and the US Department of Energy authorized an emergency exchange of up to 4 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve due to Hurricane Isaias. China also suspended oil product exports for October, further tightening global fuel markets. Shell is now expecting record refining margins due to market volatility.

**🌾 Agriculture: Harvest Headaches & USDA Shocks!**

Global agricultural commodity prices continue to face a "triple shock": Hormuz instability disrupting crucial fertilizer and energy transport, Black Sea conflicts limiting vital grain exports, and the ongoing threat of a Super El Niño. The Hormuz blockade has sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch, pushing up diammonium phosphate prices, which in turn has driven corn and soybean prices higher.

The **USDA's October WASDE report** on October 9 delivered some surprises, shockingly raising the national average corn yield to 181.2 bushels per acre and boosting production to 16.034 billion bushels, sending corn futures lower. Soybean yield was also raised slightly to 53.1 bpa, leading to record production. However, corn and wheat futures moved lower later in the week due to harvest pressure, even with delayed US fall harvests due to historic September rainfall. Brazil's soybean planting is proceeding at its fastest pace ever, while Northern Hemisphere winter wheat planting is behind pace and into dry soils.
