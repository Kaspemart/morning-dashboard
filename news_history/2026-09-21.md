**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Fed Hikes, ECB Rates Up, and Yields Soaring!**

Central banks have been busy! The **Federal Reserve hiked its key interest rate by 25 basis points to a target range of 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16th**, the first increase since July 2023, with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh signaling more hikes could be on the horizon. US Treasury yields have been soaring, with the 10-year yield hitting a staggering 5% on September 14th before easing slightly to 5.00% today. The 30-year yield is around 5.34%. Markets are now pricing in another 25 basis-point Fed rate increase by the end of 2026.

Across the pond, the **European Central Bank (ECB) hiked its key interest rate by 25 basis points to 2.50% on September 10th**, effective September 16th, to combat surging Eurozone inflation, which hit 3.3% in August. The ECB also revised up its growth forecasts. European government bond yields continued their climb, with the 10-year German Bund yield hitting a 15-year high of 3.44%. Other central banks like the Bank of Japan, Bank of Canada, and Reserve Bank of New Zealand are also on a tightening path. This week, several other central banks, including Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Mexico, and South Africa, are expected to make rate decisions, with some, like South Africa, likely to hike due to oil price inflation.

**🌾 Agriculture: WASDE Updates & Triple Shock!**

The **USDA's September WASDE report**, released on September 11th, brought notable shifts. The U.S. corn yield forecast for 2026/27 was lowered to 178.5 bushels per acre, leading to a cut in production, and the season-average farm price for corn was raised. In a surprise move, the U.S. soybean yield forecast was *increased* to 52.8 bushels per acre, pushing production close to a record high, with the season-average soybean price forecast jumping significantly. Global wheat production was raised due to gains in Australia, Canada, Ukraine, and Argentina, with the wheat season-average farm price also raised. Global agricultural commodity prices have risen noticeably since September due to a "triple shock": Hormuz instability (affecting fertilizer and energy transport), Black Sea conflicts (constraining grain exports), and expectations of a Super El Niño. Global corn consumption is forecast to exceed production by nearly 30 million metric tons in 2026/27, the largest deficit in over three decades.
