### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**🌍 Geopolitics & Conflicts: From Fragile Truce to Full-Blown Flare-Up!**
The US-Iran interim peace deal (signed June 15) officially **collapsed** this week. Following Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels in the **Strait of Hormuz** on July 7-8, the US launched retaliatory airstrikes. President Trump declared the agreement "over" and the US revoked a waiver that allowed Iranian oil sales. Iran responded with missile and drone attacks on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. The Strait, crucial for over a fifth of global seaborne oil and gas, is now subject to a reinstated US naval blockade. UN agencies have called for de-escalation, noting the "near-continuous closure" of the Strait since February has triggered significant commodity price shocks globally.
The **Venezuela earthquake disaster** continued to worsen, with the death toll rising to 4,333, and 16,740 injured and nearly 18,000 displaced from the June 24 twin earthquakes. Ukrainian attacks on approximately 90 vessels in the Sea of Azov forced Russia to halt commercial shipping and close the Don-Azov canal this week.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Hawkish Fed & Yield Swings!**
Bond markets were on a rollercoaster. Inflation reaching 4.2% (CPI) and 4.1% (PCE) in May – its highest since 2023 – initially led to a dramatic shift, with the Fed signaling one to two more rate hikes by year-end. The Fed's July Monetary Policy Report and Chairman Kevin Warsh's comments emphasized a commitment to price stability. However, June's US inflation data came in softer than expected (CPI at 3.5%, core CPI at 2.6%), leading traders to pare back July hike expectations, though a September hike remains on the table. US 10-year Treasury yields fluctuated, rising to 4.60% today. The US dollar initially strengthened amidst volatility and geopolitical risk, though it softened slightly after the softer inflation data. Eurozone bond yields also rose, with Germany's 10-year Bund yield hitting its highest since May 21, as Middle East tensions raised inflation concerns and traders priced in a September ECB hike.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: The Strait Rollercoaster!**
Oil prices experienced extreme whiplash. After initially sliding below $71 a barrel of Brent early in the week on fleeting hopes of Strait normalization, prices **surged dramatically**. By July 8, Brent crude jumped nearly 6% following tanker attacks in the Strait and Trump's declaration that the peace deal was "over." Today, Brent crude is above $86 and WTI near $80, as renewed conflict and the US naval blockade fuel intense supply concerns. Goldman Sachs noted that the next phase of Gulf flow recovery could be slower even after de-escalation, due to ongoing risk aversion by shippers.

**🌾 Agriculture: WASDE Report & Trade Hopes!**
The **USDA's July WASDE report**, released on July 10, brought key updates. New-crop US corn ending stocks were cut to 1.79 billion bushels, below trade expectations, due to increased export demand. US soybean ending stocks for new-crop were left unchanged at 310 million bushels, while US wheat ending stocks for new-crop were lowered to 722 million bushels. US wheat production for 2026 is forecast to be the smallest since 1970. China's buying of US soybeans continued throughout the week, offering some market support. The EU adopted a Delegated Act on July 13, **excluding soybean seed from deforestation regulations**, aiming to ease regulatory uncertainty.

**💰 Metals: Geopolitics & Fed Drive Swings! ✨**
Precious metals saw considerable swings. Gold initially saw gains early in the week on hopes of easing Fed rate hike fears, but then declined today due to strong resistance. Silver rebounded with a 2% gain yesterday, with platinum and palladium also climbing, likely on industrial demand. However, the renewed hostilities with Iran are expected to keep headline inflation higher, potentially tempering gold's rally, as higher inflation could prompt central banks to keep interest rates elevated.

The **collapse of the US-Iran ceasefire and escalating conflict in the Strait of Hormuz** have instantly reimposed a significant geopolitical risk premium on energy markets, directly feeding into inflation concerns that are driving the Federal Reserve's ongoing, albeit now nuanced, hawkish pivot. The sheer demand for AI infrastructure is itself becoming an inflation driver.
