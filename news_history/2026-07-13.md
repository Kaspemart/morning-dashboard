## Global Whirlwind: Hormuz in Flames, Fed Eyes More Hikes, and AI Races On! 🌍🎢🤖

### 🚨 Spotlight: Last 24-48 Hours – July 12-13, 2026!

The Middle East is back in a full-blown crisis! The shaky US-Iran ceasefire has **collapsed**, with both sides exchanging increasingly heavy blows over the weekend. The US military launched extensive airstrikes against Iran, hitting around 140 targets including missile and drone sites, while Iran retaliated by striking US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain.

This dramatic escalation has put the **Strait of Hormuz** squarely back in the global spotlight. Iran's Revolutionary Guard is firmly asserting its control, stating the Strait is "closed until further notice" and that vessels must pay fees to Tehran, a stance the US rejects as a violation of international law. Commercial shipping through this vital chokepoint is now severely disrupted.

On the financial front, the **US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.59%** today, marking a 0.02 percentage point increase from the previous session. This comes as the market continues to grapple with the Federal Reserve's hawkish signals and renewed geopolitical uncertainty. The US dollar strengthened against major currencies, contributing to a mixed performance in precious metals, where gold and silver saw slight declines today while platinum and palladium rallied due to industrial demand.

Meanwhile, the devastating twin earthquakes in Venezuela continue to be a humanitarian catastrophe, with the death toll now officially at 4,333 and over 16,740 injured.

### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**🌍 Geopolitics: From Fragile Truce to Full-Blown Flare-Up!**
The brief US-Iran interim peace deal (signed June 15) definitively ended this week. Iran attacked three commercial vessels in the **Strait of Hormuz** on July 7-8, prompting immediate retaliatory US airstrikes. President Trump declared the interim agreement "over," and the US revoked a waiver that had allowed Iranian oil sales. Iran condemned US actions and responded with missile and drone attacks on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. The Strait, crucial for over a fifth of global seaborne oil and gas, has seen renewed disruptions, and maritime authorities have raised its threat level to "severe." While US officials claim technical nuclear talks with Iran are ongoing, the overall diplomatic situation is fraught. The conflict has broad implications, with fertilizer shipments from the Middle East to sub-Saharan Africa now disrupted, potentially leading to lower crop yields and increased food insecurity.

The **Venezuela earthquake disaster** continues to unfold. The death toll from the June 24 twin earthquakes (magnitudes 7.2 and 7.5) has officially risen to 4,333, with 16,740 injured and 17,907 displaced. Over 1,170 aftershocks have been reported, and the UN has appealed for nearly $300 million to assist 1.3 million people.

In Eastern Europe, Ukrainian attacks on approximately 90 vessels in the Sea of Azov this week forced Russia to halt commercial shipping in the area, including closing the Don-Azov canal. Ukrainian drone strikes on Saturday also reportedly damaged four ships in Taganrog Bay.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Hawkish Fed & Yield Swings!**
Bond markets were on a rollercoaster this week, heavily influenced by escalating Middle East tensions and a hawkish Federal Reserve. The **Fed's July Monetary Policy Report** and comments from Chairman Kevin Warsh emphasized the FOMC's commitment to delivering price stability. Inflation reaching 4.2% (CPI) and 4.1% (PCE) – its highest since 2023 – has led to a dramatic shift, with the Fed signaling a strong possibility of one to two more rate hikes by year-end. Futures markets are now pricing in a higher probability of a September Fed hike. US 10-year Treasury yields fluctuated, reaching around 4.56% by July 10, and rising to 4.59% today, July 13, driven by inflation concerns and the hawkish Fed. The US dollar significantly strengthened amidst this volatility and renewed geopolitical risk.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Strait Back in the Spotlight!**
Oil markets experienced extreme whiplash as the US-Iran ceasefire crumbled. After an initial slide below $71 a barrel of Brent early in the week on hopes of Strait of Hormuz normalization, prices **surged dramatically**. By July 8, Brent crude jumped nearly 6% to over $80 a barrel following tanker attacks in the Strait and Trump's declaration that the peace deal was "over." US retaliatory strikes pushed Brent above $79 a barrel and WTI near $74 a barrel, with concerns growing over the security of oil shipments through the vital waterway. The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) had initially revised down its 2026 crude oil price forecast based on expectations of higher oil supply following the (now defunct) US-Iran agreement, but this outlook is clearly challenged by the renewed conflict.

**🌾 Agriculture: WASDE Report & Trade Hopes!**
The **USDA's July WASDE report**, released on Friday, July 10, brought some key updates. New-crop US corn ending stocks were cut to 1.79 billion bushels, below trade expectations. US soybean ending stocks for new-crop were left unchanged at 310 million bushels, while US wheat ending stocks for new-crop were lowered to 722 million bushels, though still slightly above trade expectations. USDA also raised US soybean production slightly to 4.475 billion bushels, which would be a US production record if it holds. China's buying of US soybeans continued throughout the week, offering some support to the market, though the broader market for US corn and soybeans is looking for stronger export demand. The wheat complex saw strength on Friday, driven more by geopolitical news (Kerch Strait closure reports) than the WASDE data.

**💰 Metals: Geopolitics & Fed Drive Swings! ✨**
Precious metals saw considerable swings this week. Gold and silver initially saw gains early in the week on hopes of easing Fed rate hike fears, but this reversed by mid-week. By July 10, gold and silver registered slight declines as the US-Iran ceasefire collapsed and the US dollar strengthened. Platinum and palladium, however, moved in the opposite direction, climbing due to distinct industrial-demand dynamics, largely separate from the dollar and interest rate narrative impacting gold and silver. The heightened geopolitical risk from the Strait of Hormuz conflict is viewed as a "structural constraint on global oil supply that keeps inflation expectations elevated," making physical gold and silver attractive despite short-term price movements influenced by Fed rate hike fears.

**🤖 Tech & AI Frontier: Chips, Clouds & Geopolitical Scrutiny!**
In the AI world, the **US government lifted export restrictions on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 AI models** on July 1, allowing for wider global distribution after safety concerns were addressed. CIA Director John Ratcliffe reiterated warnings about AI being "digital nuclear weapons," underscoring the urgency for the agency's adoption of AI and quantum computing. OpenAI is reportedly preparing a confidential IPO filing with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, targeting a public debut as early as September 2026 at a private-market valuation around $730 billion. However, Anthropic has reportedly overtaken OpenAI in revenue, running at approximately $47 billion annualized against OpenAI's projected $25-$33 billion for 2026, largely driven by its enterprise engine. Nvidia also reclaimed a $5 trillion market capitalization, capping a week where semiconductor names generally outperformed AI software companies. Concerns about "distillation" – using one AI model's outputs to improve another – are growing, with Anthropic warning that competitors are harvesting its outputs at scale. This highlights the intense competition and IP challenges in the AI space.

### Broader Implications: Return of Volatility 🎲

This past week has been a stark reminder that global stability is incredibly fragile. The **collapse of the US-Iran ceasefire and escalating conflict in the Strait of Hormuz** have instantly reimposed a significant geopolitical risk premium on energy markets, directly feeding into inflation concerns that are driving the Federal Reserve's hawkish pivot. This dramatic interplay of renewed conflict and aggressive monetary policy will ensure continued **volatility for US Treasuries, European bonds, and FX markets**, demanding heightened vigilance from traders. The underlying inflation pressure from energy supply disruptions, despite some agricultural good news, remains a key driver for central bank actions. Meanwhile, the strategic competition for critical minerals, the growing concerns about AI "distillation," and the geopolitical framing of AI technology underscore a shifting global landscape where economic and technological dominance are increasingly intertwined with national security concerns.
