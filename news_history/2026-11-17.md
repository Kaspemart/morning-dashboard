**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Inflation's Stubborn Grip & Yield Swings!**

The **US Consumer Price Index (CPI) for October**, released on November 10, came in hotter than expected, with headline CPI up 0.4% month-over-month and core inflation increasing 0.3%. This immediately sent **US Treasury yields** surging across the curve, with the 10-year hitting 4.99% and the 2-year seeing its largest daily increase since April 2025. The **US dollar** strengthened significantly as markets now fully price in a December Fed rate hike, with an 11% chance of a 50-basis-point increase. **European bond yields** also climbed, and the probability of a December ECB rate hike jumped to around 75%. The US Producer Price Index (PPI) for October was released on November 13, also a key gauge for inflationary pressures.

Both the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank (ECB) had held interest rates steady last week, but the fresh inflation data has drastically shifted expectations for future tightening. The Bank of England, however, bucked the trend by hiking its interest rate by 25 basis points to 4% last week. ECB President Christine Lagarde announced her early departure in February 2027, adding a layer of uncertainty.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hustle & Surplus Concerns!**

The escalating attacks in the **Strait of Hormuz** continued to fuel fears of broader Middle East conflict and disrupt supply, making oil prices highly volatile. Despite these tensions, the International Energy Agency (IEA) issued a report on November 13, forecasting that the global oil market is expected to return to a surplus in Q4 2026 for the first time since the Iran war began, driven by recovering supply and weaker demand. IEA also projected a larger market surplus in 2027, but warned that further attacks on shipping could undermine the recovery. Global oil demand is projected to decline in 2026 before recovering in 2027, with high fuel prices and vehicle electrification limiting consumption growth in the transport sector. US commercial crude inventories saw an unexpected increase of 4.7 million barrels in the week ended November 6.

**🌾 Agriculture: Triple Shock Continues, WASDE Surprises, & Trade Deals!**

Global agricultural markets remain on a "triple shock" treadmill: **Hormuz instability** snarls crucial fertilizer and energy transport, **Black Sea conflicts** limit vital grain exports, and the looming threat of a **Super El Niño** adds climate uncertainty. The Hormuz blockade has particularly triggered a severe global fertilizer supply crunch. The USDA's November WASDE report (November 10) provided surprises, cutting the national corn yield and trimming soybean ending stocks due to robust export demand, though world wheat stocks reportedly grew. Ukrainian corn export prices have fallen due to increasing global stocks and weakening demand. Canada substantially concluded negotiations for a free trade agreement with ASEAN, a deal expected to add nearly $2 billion to Canada's GDP and create 14,000 jobs, particularly in agriculture and manufacturing. Oregon confirmed its first case of bird flu in dairy cattle on November 10, prompting a farm quarantine.
