The conflict dangerously expanded this week, with Iran-backed **Houthi forces in Yemen attacking Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea** and announcing a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia, causing some tankers to make U-turns. **💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Tightrope Walks and Hawkish Pauses!**
The **Federal Reserve** has been on a wild ride of expectations. May's higher CPI (4.2%) and PCE (4.1%) initially suggested more rate hikes, but then came cooler June inflation data (CPI eased to 3.5%, core CPI to 2.6%, and PPI actually *fell* 0.3%). This caused traders to largely unwind bets on a July Fed hike, though a September hike remains a strong possibility (around 55%). Fed Chair Kevin Warsh acknowledged that massive AI investments might drive up short-term prices but isn't convinced it means persistent inflation. US 10-year Treasury yields hovered around 4.63% this week, reflecting geopolitical drama and evolving Fed expectations. The **US Dollar Index (DXY)** held firm, bolstered by Mideast risks. Across the pond, the **European Central Bank (ECB)** is expected to hold rates today but is signaling a "hawkish pause," ready for a potential September hike if energy price surges fuel inflation. Eurozone bond yields also saw a bump due to the Middle East conflict.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hijinks Fuel Supply Fears!**
Oil prices have been a direct reflection of the escalating Strait of Hormuz conflict. After a brief dip on fleeting peace hopes, prices exploded, with **Brent crude reaching two-month highs** and WTI following suit. The US naval blockade and intensifying conflict in the Strait, compounded by Houthi attacks in the Red Sea, are fueling massive supply concerns. Goldman Sachs even warned Brent crude could top $120 if disruptions persist. The IEA head is sounding the alarm on global energy security.

**🌾 Agriculture: War, Weather, and Research Woes!**
The **USDA's July WASDE report** brought mixed signals: new-crop US corn ending stocks were cut due to increased export demand, soybean ending stocks held steady (with a projected record US production), and **US wheat ending stocks were lowered**, pointing to the smallest US wheat production since 1970. China continued its appetite for US soybeans. The EU adopted a Delegated Act to exclude soybean seed from deforestation regulations. Hot weather in the US Midwest remains a concern for corn and soybeans. Meanwhile, the escalating **Black Sea conflict** has severely impacted Ukrainian grain exports, keeping wheat prices elevated. Concerns are growing over the **decline in public investment in US agricultural research** since 2002, contrasting with China's booming investment in this sector.

**💰 Metals: Navigating the Headwinds! ✨**
Precious metals saw a volatile week. Gold and silver initially gained on hopes of easing Fed rate hikes but then experienced declines, caught between rising oil prices (geopolitical risk) and the Fed's ongoing hawkish stance (despite softer CPI/PPI). Platinum and palladium, driven more by industrial demand, showed mixed movements. Central banks, however, are reportedly still keen buyers on price dips. China maintains its tight grip on rare earth export controls. **Guardian Metal's Pilot Mountain Tungsten Project** in Nevada, backed by US Defense Production Act investment, released positive Pre-Feasibility Study results, signaling a potential new US tungsten source.
