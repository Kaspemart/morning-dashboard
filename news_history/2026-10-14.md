**US Treasury yields** have been on a wild ride. After surging to multi-decade highs earlier in the week (10-year yield near 5.3% and 30-year near 5.66%) due to persistent inflation concerns and strong US economic data, they saw some pullback. This easing followed signals from Federal Reserve officials hinting at less urgency for immediate rate hikes, especially after a weaker-than-expected September US non-farm payrolls report. Despite this, the Fed's latest minutes still suggest most officials foresee another rate hike *this year*, with markets pricing in an 86% probability of a December hike. Crucial US inflation data is expected later this week. **European government bond yields** similarly climbed, with French 10-year bond yields nearing a 24-year high due to concerns over France's debt burden and political stability. **🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hustle, Ceasefire Claims, & Supply Shifts!**

**Oil prices** were a dizzying rollercoaster this week. They initially surged past $108 a barrel due to escalating Hormuz tensions and tanker attacks, before dipping on President Trump's "energy ceasefire" claims. Brent crude remains above $100. **🌾 Agriculture: Harvest Headaches, USDA Updates, & Fertilizer Frenzy!**

Global agricultural commodity prices continue to face a "triple shock": Hormuz instability disrupting fertilizer and energy transport, Black Sea conflicts limiting grain exports, and the ongoing threat of a Super El Niño. The Hormuz blockade has sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch, driving up diammonium phosphate prices, which in turn has pushed up corn and soybean prices. Farmers are reportedly reducing fertilizer applications, which could impact future global yields. The **USDA's October WASDE report** on October 9 delivered some surprises, shockingly raising the national average corn yield to 181.2 bushels per acre and boosting production to 16.034 billion bushels, sending corn futures lower. Soybean yield was also raised slightly to 53.1 bpa, leading to record production. However, corn and wheat futures moved lower later in the week due to harvest pressure, while soybeans initially saw a bearish reaction before rallying. Delayed US fall harvests due to historic September rainfall remain a concern, even with recent favorable weather in the Midwest helping catch up.
