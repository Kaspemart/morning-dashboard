### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Inflation's Jolt & Yield Uncertainty!**

**US Treasury yields** have been on a volatile ride. Last week saw them pull back from multi-decade highs, but a "hotter-than-expected" US CPI report on October 14, showing consumer prices surging in September (driven largely by a jump in gasoline prices), initially injected renewed hawkishness into central bank expectations. However, subsequent caution from Federal Reserve officials, including Chairman Kevin Warsh, hinting they can wait before acting further in October, caused yields to pull back slightly, before seeing a slight uptick today. Markets are now closely watching upcoming US producer price and retail sales data. The Fed Board also held closed meetings on financial markets and discount rates.

Across the Atlantic, **European government bond yields** eased somewhat last week, with Italian BTP yields pulling back as an initial "energy truce" announcement improved sentiment. However, concerns persist, particularly in France, where 30-year bond spreads over German Bunds have widened, driven by high deficits, rising yields, and political uncertainty. The European Commission successfully raised €10 billion in EU-Bonds last week, with demand surging to historic highs.

**Oil prices** were a dizzying rollercoaster this week, initially surging due to escalating Hormuz tensions and tanker attacks, before dipping today on President Trump's waiver of sanctions on Russia. European natural gas prices also climbed to a nearly four-year high, fueled by Middle East attacks and low storage levels.

**🌾 Agriculture: Harvest Headaches & Fertilizer Frenzy!**

Global agricultural commodity prices continue to face a "triple shock": ongoing instability in the Strait of Hormuz disrupting crucial fertilizer and energy transport, conflicts in the Black Sea limiting vital grain exports, and the looming threat of a Super El Niño. The Hormuz blockade has sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch, driving up diammonium phosphate prices, which in turn has pushed up corn and soybean prices. A UN agency warned this week that Hormuz shipping disruption is already hitting harvests and threatening food security.

The **USDA's October WASDE report** on October 9 delivered some surprises, shockingly raising the national average corn yield and boosting production, initially sending corn futures lower. Soybean yield was also raised slightly, leading to record production. However, corn and wheat futures moved lower later in the week due to harvest pressure, even with delayed US fall harvests due to historic September rainfall. Brazil's soybean planting is proceeding at its fastest pace ever, while Northern Hemisphere winter wheat planting is behind pace and into dry soils.
