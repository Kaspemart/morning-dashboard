### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

The **Strait of Hormuz** has been a raging inferno, with tanker attacks surging to a multi-month high, dramatically slowing vessel traffic to a two-month low earlier in the week. Last week, a Kuwaiti supertanker was hit, and this week saw an LPG tanker struck off Oman on October 6, injuring 12 crew, and another off Qatar on October 7, causing unspecified casualties. Iran has threatened to close alternative routes and rejected the latest US proposal to reopen the Strait, demanding seven conditions be met. President Trump had initially rejected Iran's ceasefire offers and was reportedly considering pre-election strikes, but this weekend indicated "productive discussions" were underway and that the US would *not* attack Iran before the midterms. Despite official blockades, some crude oil flows reportedly increased through workarounds, but refined product shipments remain severely hampered. Adding to the Middle East volatility, Houthi rebels have significantly escalated drone and missile attacks on Saudi airports, with deadly strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend. The US is weighing joining Saudi strikes against the Houthis, and Canada has advised against non-essential travel to Saudi Arabia.

In the relentless **Ukraine conflict**, Russia continued its brutal missile and drone barrages. On October 7, a massive assault killed 22 people, including five children, in Pryluky, and two in Kyiv. Russian strikes on Saturday killed 23 people, including four children, in Zaporizhzhia, and two were killed in Kharkiv on Sunday. Ukrainian forces reported small gains in Donetsk and counterattacks in Sumy. Ukraine also carried out drone strikes on Russian oil infrastructure in Rostov Oblast on October 10 and hit a Yandex data center in Ryazan region, causing outages across Russian cities. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy continues to report large numbers of drones and missiles fired by Russia. The Kremlin acknowledged a "stalemate" but dismissed immediate peace summit prospects. Germany accused "Ukrainian state authorities" of ordering the 2022 Nord Stream pipeline sabotage, and NATO condemned Russia's "irresponsible nuclear rhetoric."

Away from these hot zones, a **terror scare in the UK** saw seven men arrested near a US-operated air base, with investigations into potential links to Iran or Russia. North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles towards the East Sea today. Hurricane Isaias tore a deadly path through Florida and Alabama, causing wreckage and production shut-ins in the US Gulf of Mexico, with Chevron gradually restoring operations. Hurricane Simon, a Category 4 storm, also battered Mexico's coast. A food charity in Toronto reported seven of its trucks vandalized over Thanksgiving weekend, with catalytic converters stolen, impacting food deliveries.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Yields' Rollercoaster & Inflation Watch!**

**US Treasury yields** were on a wild ride, surging to multi-decade highs earlier in the week, with the 10-year yield around 5.3% and the 30-year near 5.66%. This was fueled by persistent inflation concerns and strong US economic data. However, yields saw some pullback later in the week after Federal Reserve officials hinted at less urgency for immediate rate hikes, especially following a weaker-than-expected September US non-farm payrolls report. This week brings crucial US inflation data.

**European government bond yields** similarly climbed, with French 10-year bond yields nearing a 24-year high due to concerns over France's debt burden and political stability. The euro hit a 17-month low against the dollar before recovering slightly. German bonds benefited from their safe-haven status.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hustle, Ceasefire Claims, & Supply Shifts!**

**Oil prices** have been a dizzying rollercoaster, initially surging past $108 a barrel due to escalating Hormuz tensions and tanker attacks, before dipping today on President Trump's energy ceasefire claims. Brent crude remains above $100. The US has lifted sanctions on the sale of Russian diesel until April next year amidst an unprecedented price surge. **🌾 Agriculture: Harvest Headaches, USDA Updates, & Fertilizer Frenzy!**

Global agricultural commodity prices continue to face a "triple shock": Hormuz instability disrupting fertilizer and energy transport, Black Sea conflicts limiting grain exports, and the ongoing threat of a Super El Niño. The **Hormuz blockade sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch**, driving up diammonium phosphate prices by 28.4% year-to-date, which has consequently pushed up corn prices by 13.3% and soybeans by 22.6%. Farmers are reportedly reducing fertilizer applications.

The **USDA's October WASDE report** on Friday, October 9, delivered some surprises. It shockingly raised the national average corn yield to 181.2 bushels per acre, the largest October increase since 2005, and boosted production to 16.034 billion bushels, sending corn futures lower. Soybean yield was also raised slightly to 53.1 bpa, leading to record production. Wheat ending stocks rose due to slightly higher production and lower exports. However, corn and wheat futures moved lower later in the week due to harvest pressure, while soybeans initially saw a bearish reaction before rallying as traders covered shorts. Delayed US fall harvests due to historic September rainfall also remain a concern.

The **AI safety debate** is intensifying. Executives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Google testified before the New York City Council last week, unable to guarantee their AI models would always adhere to safety protocols. The **US and China formalized a "US-China Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue"** to flag national-security-level AI incidents. The IMF noted today that AI investment is helping sustain global economic growth at around 3%, with Asian economies benefiting from the demand for AI hardware.
