### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**🌍 Geopolitics & Conflicts: The Strait of Flames Ignites!**
The brief US-Iran interim peace deal? Long gone. Following Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the **Strait of Hormuz** (July 7-8), the US launched immediate retaliatory airstrikes. President Trump declared the agreement "over" and promptly **reinstated a naval blockade on all Iranian ports**, warning of further strikes if negotiations don't resume. Iran has been quick to retaliate with missile and drone attacks on US bases across Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan. The Strait, a global energy chokepoint responsible for 34% of globally traded crude oil and 20% of LNG, is at a near-standstill for commercial shipping, with only nine vessels passing through on Wednesday, down from 13 the previous day. The UN warns the full economic impact of these disruptions won't be clear until late 2026. Despite the intense military action, some hopes for diplomacy remain, with mediators from Pakistan, Qatar, and Egypt continuing efforts to bring both sides back to the negotiating table.

Elsewhere, the **Venezuela earthquake disaster** continues its tragic toll, with fatalities officially at 4,333 and a UN appeal for nearly $300 million in aid for the 1.3 million affected people. Ukrainian attacks on Russian vessels in the **Sea of Azov** have forced Russia to halt commercial shipping there, further escalating global trade woes.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: The Fed's Tightrope Walk!**
The **Federal Reserve** had markets on a rollercoaster. Earlier in the week, May's CPI (4.2%) and PCE (4.1%) hinted at more rate hikes, with the Fed signaling one to two more by year-end. But then, June brought a pleasant surprise: headline CPI eased to 3.5%, core CPI to 2.6%, and the PPI *fell* by 0.3%. This shift has traders largely unwinding bets on a July rate hike, though a September hike remains a strong possibility (around 44-49%). Fed Chair Kevin Warsh acknowledged that massive AI investments might drive up short-term prices for tech and energy, but he's not convinced it will lead to persistent inflation. US 10-year Treasury yields climbed to a two-month high of 4.63% earlier in the week before settling around 4.56% today, responding to the geopolitical drama and evolving Fed expectations. The **US dollar** initially strengthened with the Mideast jitters, but softened slightly after the softer inflation data. Across the pond, **Eurozone bond yields** also saw a bump, with Germany's 10-year Bund hitting its highest since May, as the Middle East conflict raised inflation concerns.

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Hijinks Fuel Supply Fears!**
Oil prices have been on a wild ride, surging and dipping with every headline out of the Strait of Hormuz. After a brief dip below $71 a barrel (Brent) on fleeting peace hopes, prices exploded. The reinstated US naval blockade and intensifying conflict have pushed Brent above $85 and WTI over $80, marking a one-month high. Analysts are warning that a prolonged "Strait impasse" could send Brent soaring well over $110 a barrel. The IMF also warned that the global economy has less capacity to cushion the blow from reduced energy supplies from the Strait.

**🌾 Agriculture: War, Weather, and Trade Winds!**
The **USDA's July WASDE report** (July 10) brought mixed news: new-crop US corn ending stocks were cut due to increased export demand, soybean ending stocks held steady (with a projected record US production), and US wheat ending stocks were lowered, pointing to the smallest US wheat production since 1970. China continued its appetite for US soybeans. The EU adopted a Delegated Act to **exclude soybean seed from deforestation regulations**, easing concerns for European protein crops. On a supportive note, the USDA is holding a webinar today for its $500 million FIELDS program to boost domestic fertilizer production. However, hot weather in the US Midwest is closely watched for its impact on corn and soybeans. The escalating conflict in the **Black Sea** has severely impacted Ukrainian grain exports, with Ukraine losing about a third of its grain export capacity through its Black Sea ports, causing wheat prices to soar to all-time highs. Concerns over weak export demand also hit US grain markets on Thursday, with corn, soybeans, and wheat turning lower despite earlier gains.

**💰 Metals: Navigating the Headwinds! ✨**
Precious metals had a volatile week. Gold and silver initially gained on hopes of easing Fed rate hikes but then saw declines today, with gold futures down 1.00% to $3990.10 and silver futures down 2.91% to $55.45, as rising oil prices (geopolitical risk) and the Fed's ongoing hawkish tone (despite softer CPI/PPI) created headwinds. Platinum and palladium, driven more by industrial demand, showed mixed movements, with platinum up 0.41% to $1,650.70 and palladium up 0.41% to $1,342.00 today. Central banks, however, are reportedly still keen buyers on price dips. In industrial metals, China maintains its tight grip on rare earth export controls, while **Guardian Metal's Pilot Mountain Tungsten Project** in Nevada, backed by US Defense Production Act investment, released positive Pre-Feasibility Study results, signaling a potential new US tungsten source after over a decade.

The AI race is full throttle! The US government lifted export restrictions on **Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 AI models** earlier this month, clearing them for wider global distribution. OpenAI is reportedly eyeing a confidential IPO at a staggering $730 billion valuation, though Anthropic is said to have overtaken it in revenue. Nvidia reclaimed a $5 trillion market cap. Intel announced a massive **€5 billion investment in Ireland** to expand AI chip production, reflecting surging global demand, which also boosted South Korea's economic growth forecast. Intel is also expanding its collaboration with Google Cloud to integrate Gemini Enterprise across its global workforce. Concerns are also growing about "distillation"—where one AI model learns from another's outputs. On the policy front, CIA Director John Ratcliffe warned of AI as "digital nuclear weapons," while Japan revised its AI Basic Plan to bolster cyberattack defenses. China's President Xi Jinping delivered a keynote speech at the World AI Conference today. Notably, Fed Chair Warsh highlighted that the massive AI buildout is a new inflation threat, driving up costs for memory chips, processors, and electricity. Regulatory hurdles are also becoming increasingly common, as seen with Apple's need to partner with Chinese models like Alibaba's Qwen and Baidu for its AI features in mainland China.

### Broader Implications: Navigating the Whiplash! 🎲

This week has been a masterclass in market whiplash, demonstrating the extreme fragility of global stability. The **escalating US-Iran conflict and the near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz** have instantly reimposed a significant geopolitical risk premium on energy markets, directly feeding into inflation concerns that are keeping central banks, particularly the Federal Reserve, on high alert despite recent softer data. This dramatic interplay of renewed conflict and monetary policy uncertainty ensures continued **volatility for US Treasuries, European bonds, and FX markets**. The underlying inflation pressure from energy supply disruptions, along with the impact of the Black Sea conflict on agricultural commodities, remains a key driver for central bank actions. Meanwhile, the AI revolution charges ahead, but its swift integration is hitting intellectual property roadblocks, revealing a bifurcating global tech landscape, and exposing critical infrastructure needs like energy, becoming an inflation driver in itself. The global market is playing a high-stakes game where geopolitical stability, monetary policy, and transformative technology are all interconnected and prone to sudden shifts, creating both immense risk and fleeting opportunities.
