**US Treasury yields** have been on a volatile ride. After pulling back from multi-decade highs, a "hotter-than-expected" US CPI report on October 14, showing consumer prices surging 3.7% in September (driven largely by a 9.1% jump in gasoline prices), initially injected renewed hawkishness into central bank expectations. However, Federal Reserve officials, including Chairman Kevin Warsh, signaled caution, hinting they can wait before acting further in October, which initially tempered hike bets. Today's weaker US housing starts data also contributed to lower long-term yields. Markets are now closely watching upcoming US producer price and retail sales data, with a December rate hike still strongly anticipated. The US Treasury is actively selling bills and 5-year TIPS.

Across the Atlantic, **European government bond yields** eased somewhat last week, but concerns persist, particularly in France, where 30-year bond spreads over German Bunds have widened due to high deficits and political uncertainty.

**🌾 Agriculture: Harvest Headaches & Fertilizer Frenzy!**

Global agricultural commodity prices continue to face a "triple shock": ongoing instability in the Strait of Hormuz disrupting crucial fertilizer and energy transport, conflicts in the Black Sea limiting vital grain exports, and the looming threat of a Super El Niño. The Hormuz blockade has sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch, driving up diammonium phosphate prices, which in turn has pushed up corn and soybean prices. A UN agency warned this week that Hormuz shipping disruption is already hitting harvests and threatening food security.

The **USDA's October WASDE report** on October 9 delivered some surprises, surprisingly raising the national average corn yield and boosting production, initially sending corn futures lower. Soybean yield was also raised slightly, leading to record production. However, corn and **wheat futures** moved lower later in the week due to harvest pressure and weak exports. Delayed US fall harvests due to historic September rainfall remain a concern, even as Brazil's soybean planting proceeds at its fastest pace ever. President Trump also announced an $8 billion "Harvest Fuel Relief" program to aid farmers with high input costs.
