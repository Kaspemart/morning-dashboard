### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

**🌍 Geopolitics & Conflicts: Summit Diplomacy, Hormuz Inferno & Ukraine's Resilience!**

President Trump’s diplomatic swing through Asia culminated in the **historic trilateral summit** with Xi and Putin, following his meeting with Quad leaders on November 17. The unprecedented gathering aimed to discuss nuclear arms, Iran, and Ukraine, ultimately producing a "peace declaration" focused on broader global stability rather than immediate conflict resolutions.

The **Strait of Hormuz** continued its chaotic run, with a Panama-flagged LPG tanker hit by a missile on November 16, and Iran’s IRGC Navy claiming a *fourth* oil tanker caught fire. Unverified Iranian claims also reported two supertankers hitting mines and one sinking. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) has reported 133 confirmed commercial vessel incidents and 25 seafarers killed in the region since the conflict began. Iran continues to assert "full control" and targets vessels using "unauthorized routes." The US responded with airstrikes on Iran's Qeshm island in retaliation for targeting US assets.

In the relentless **Ukraine conflict**, Ukrainian forces celebrated an "unprecedented operation," striking the vital Kerch Bridge on November 16-17 with "Palianytsia drones" and "Neptune missiles," causing significant damage to both road and railway sections. Russia retaliated with widespread missile and drone attacks, hitting critical energy infrastructure across Ukraine, causing power outages, and damaging Kyiv's Justice Ministry headquarters and bridges. Russian drones even violated Moldovan airspace. Earlier US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi (November 10-11) wrapped up with an agreement in principle for a prisoner exchange, but no major breakthrough on ending the war, as Europe now seeks a seat at the table for future discussions. Russia, however, remains firm against NATO membership for Ukraine and European involvement in talks.

**💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Inflation's Grip, Yield Swings, & Retail Reality!**

The **US economy** got a fresh look this week with the release of **October's Advance Monthly Retail Sales** on November 17. Total retail and food services sales rose 0.2% month-over-month and a solid 6.8% year-over-year, with core retail sales up 0.6%. This signals continued, albeit moderate, consumer demand. This data follows **October's hotter-than-expected US CPI report** (November 10), which solidified market expectations for a December Fed rate hike. US Treasury yields surged in response, with the 10-year yield breaking above 5%, and the US dollar strengthened significantly. European bond yields also climbed, increasing the probability of a December ECB rate hike to around 75%. The Bank of England had already bucked the trend last week, hiking its interest rate by 25 basis points to 4%. [cite: previous briefings]

**🛢️ Oil & Energy: Hormuz Chaos vs. Supply Surplus Hopes!**

The ongoing attacks in the **Strait of Hormuz** continue to inject volatility and supply-risk premiums into oil markets. However, despite these tensions, a report from the International Energy Agency (IEA) on November 13 forecasted that the global oil market would actually return to a surplus in Q4 2026, with a larger surplus expected in 2027, driven by recovering Middle East supply and weaker demand. Goldman Sachs echoed this on November 15, projecting a "sizeable surplus" into 2027, which has helped cap some oil price gains. The IEA still warns that global **diesel supplies will remain "tight" into 2027**. US commercial crude inventories saw an unexpected increase in the week ended November 6, while the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at a 1982 low. Goldman Sachs also anticipates China's oil demand peaked in 2025, driven by weakening ground transportation fuel use.

**🌾 Agriculture: Triple Threat & Trade Talk!**

Global agricultural markets remain on a "triple shock" treadmill: **Hormuz instability** snarls crucial fertilizer and energy transport, **Black Sea conflicts** continue to limit vital grain exports, and the looming threat of a **Super El Niño** adds climate uncertainty. A drone strike tragically sank a wheat cargo off Bulgaria, adding fresh risk premiums to Black Sea shipments. The USDA's November 10 WASDE report surprised markets by cutting the national corn yield and trimming soybean ending stocks due to robust export demand, though world wheat stocks reportedly grew. China's slow follow-through on its pledged $17 billion annual US farm purchases continues to create demand uncertainty for corn, even as hopes linger for Beijing to drop tariffs on US soybeans. Meanwhile, Polish grain elevators halted wet corn intake due to a propane shortage, and Oregon confirmed its first case of bird flu in dairy cattle on November 10. The International Coconut Community also held a ministerial meeting in Kochi, India, from November 17-20, focusing on higher-value coconut products.

**💰 Metals: Inflation's Shadow Lingers! ✨**

Precious metals continued to face headwinds this week from the "hot" US CPI data and elevated Treasury yields, making non-yielding assets less attractive. Gold traded below $3,900, with silver sliding below $46 an ounce and platinum below $1,400 earlier in the week. The global silver market is forecast to record its sixth consecutive annual deficit in 2026.

**🤖 Tech & AI Frontier: Nvidia's Shake-Up & Chip Control!**

The **AI industry** is buzzing with the ongoing drama at **Nvidia**. Following Jensen Huang's resignation on November 12, Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO, and the company's board has officially stated it does not plan to reinstate Huang. Nvidia’s board also established a permanent committee for national security and export controls on November 17, reflecting the company's deep ties to US foreign policy.

Meanwhile, the US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) announced an interim final rule on November 16, which was scheduled for Federal Register publication on November 19. This rule expands scrutiny of overseas data centers and adds licensing requirements for remote access to advanced AI chips in certain countries, aiming to prevent circumvention of existing export controls. This comes as China is reportedly distilling OpenAI outputs to train its own AI models. The AI industry also faces legal challenges: US nonprofits filed a lawsuit on November 12 against major tech giants (Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, SpaceX) demanding a freeze on AI model training due to "catastrophic harm" concerns, and the California AG sued OpenAI over a data breach and "loss of control" incidents. Despite these headwinds, Anthropic plans a massive $26 billion private placement with tech partners (including Google, Nvidia, AMD, Microsoft) alongside its IPO.
