### The Week That Was: A Geopolitical Soap Opera & Tech Tidal Wave! 🎭

In the relentless **Ukraine conflict**, there's been a grim dance between diplomacy and destruction. Beyond these hotspots, **France** remains gripped by nationwide protests over rising living costs and poor school conditions, leading to widespread rioting and looting. Italy is also planning troop deployments amid widening protests.

This reinforces concerns that soaring energy costs are feeding broader inflationary pressures.

Adding a twist to European policy, ECB President Christine Lagarde announced her early departure in February 2027, though she stressed that rising energy prices continue to pose upside risks to inflation and downside risks to growth. Eurozone countries have until Friday to nominate her successor. European government bond yields, particularly French 10-year yields, remained under pressure, nearing 5% amidst ongoing fiscal concerns and political instability, especially as France's GDP actually contracted in Q3.

In **FX markets**, the US dollar generally strengthened over the week, benefiting from Euro and Yen weakness, though it softened slightly at week's end.

**🌾 Agriculture: Soybean Strength, Corn Questions, & Harvest Relief!**

Global agricultural commodity markets are navigating a "triple shock" from Hormuz instability (disrupting crucial fertilizer and energy transport), Black Sea conflicts (limiting vital grain exports), and the looming threat of a Super El Niño. The Hormuz blockade has sparked a severe global fertilizer supply crunch, pushing up prices for diammonium phosphate, which in turn boosts corn and soybean prices. The USDA's October WASDE report surprisingly raised national average corn and soybean yields.

**Soybeans** saw a strong rally last week, thanks to robust US export sales and renewed Chinese buying, even during the US harvest season. US soybean harvest is 88% complete. In contrast, **corn and wheat futures** moved lower, largely due to seasonal harvest pressure in the US (corn harvest is 74% complete) and an easing of geopolitical risk premiums, despite ongoing Black Sea uncertainties. The Trump administration rolled out a hefty $15 billion "Harvest Fuel Relief" program to aid farmers with high input costs. Nigeria, a major wheat importer, expects its wheat imports to decline due to inflation, insecurity, and high input costs, further impacting global grain trade.
