## Global Whirlwind: Tariffs Erupt, Hormuz on Fire, Inflation Heats Up & Nvidia's CEO is BACK! 💥💸🤖

### 🚨 Spotlight: Last 24-48 Hours – November 25-26, 2026!

Talk about a busy mid-week! The **US Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index** for October, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, dropped on Wednesday, showing prices heating up. Headline PCE rose 0.3% month-over-month and a scorching 3.8% year-over-year. Even "core" PCE (stripping out food and energy) was up 0.3% MoM and 3.3% YoY. Both numbers accelerated from September, with core PCE hitting a three-year high, solidifying expectations for another Fed rate hike in December.

Meanwhile, President Trump's administration decided it was time to shake up global trade, proposing **new tariffs on imports from dozens of countries** on Wednesday, including a 20% tariff on China (which could jump to 40%) and 15% on the EU (potentially 25%). Predictably, China wasn't thrilled, warning it would take "necessary measures" in response.

In the tech universe, the US authorized **Elon Musk's SpaceX** to launch up to a million satellites by 2040 to create orbital data centers – a huge move for AI infrastructure! Nvidia will be supplying its Vera Rubin processors for this ambitious project.

The **Ukraine conflict** continues to simmer, with Russia vowing retaliation for Ukrainian refinery strikes. Italy also raised an eyebrow, warning the conflict has spread to the Mediterranean after a Ukrainian drone reportedly struck a Russian ship in Benghazi, Libya, killing two Libyan naval officers.

And in some big European news, the EU and UK slapped major fragrance firms with **over 570 million euros in fines** on Wednesday for running an international cartel that likely inflated consumer prices.

The **Strait of Hormuz** has been nothing short of a raging inferno, with attacks on shipping reaching their highest levels since the Iran war began (over 30 incidents in October, at least 22 so far in November). Iran's IRGC Navy kept up its claims of tankers hitting naval mines, often without independent verification, while the US confirmed Iran is deploying anti-ship cruise missiles. The **Ukraine conflict** saw a significant escalation with Russia launching one of its largest missile and drone strikes this month on November 19-20, hitting critical infrastructure and bridges in Kyiv. Shockingly, Russian missiles and drones intentionally flew into Polish airspace during these attacks, with two drones even violating Moldovan airspace. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy, for his part, defended strikes on Russian energy sites, expanding Ukraine's operational reach. The UN's World Food Programme warned that the war is severely disrupting grain shipments, jeopardizing food aid for millions globally.

On the diplomatic front, President Trump's historic trilateral summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on November 19 produced a "peace declaration" but no immediate breakthroughs on Ukraine or Iran. **💰 Market Movers & Central Bank Whispers: Inflation's Stubborn Grip & Consumer's Smile!**

The **US economy** remains the focus. Beyond this week's PCE data, **US Consumer Confidence** surged 11.4 points to 103.3 in November – its highest since January 2025 and well above expectations! This boost in optimism, possibly linked to reduced post-midterm election uncertainty, paints a rosier picture for consumer spending heading into the holidays. However, consumers still expressed frustration with high prices and rising costs.

The latest **US Flash PMIs** for November, released on Monday, eased slightly to a four-month low of 54.6, with services and manufacturing growth slowing. Despite this moderation, the PMI still signals robust economic growth (around 3% GDP in Q4) and solid employment gains. Input cost inflation cooled, partly due to lower fuel prices, but "remained elevated." For **US Treasuries and FX**, the hot PCE data and solid consumer confidence strongly reinforce market expectations for a December Fed rate hike, likely putting upward pressure on yields and the dollar. European bonds will also react to the global inflationary pressures.

### Broader Implications: The Volatility Treadmill Accelerates 🎲

The global stage continues its relentless spin on a volatility treadmill, now dramatically accelerated by stubbornly hot **US inflation data**, President Trump's aggressive new **tariff proposals**, and the persistent **Strait of Hormuz crisis**. For market participants in **US Treasuries, European bonds, and FX**, the interplay of entrenched inflation, increasingly hawkish central bank stances, and sovereign debt concerns remains paramount. The AI revolution, while driving unprecedented innovation, is simultaneously navigating significant leadership changes, mounting national security concerns over chip access, and increasing legal and public scrutiny, all of which could temper future tech investment. Energy markets are caught in a tug-of-war between persistent supply disruptions from the Middle East and forecasts for a global surplus, while agriculture contends with multiple shocks and uncertain trade relations, particularly with China, which could be further complicated by the new US tariffs.
